## FAQ

### What is You Owe Me?

You Owe Me is an iPhone app for tracking money between people. It helps with IOUs, shared bills, separate informal loans, repayment plans that follow real payments, recurring costs, reminders, Live Links, PDF statements, and follow-up messages.

### Is You Owe Me available on Android?

No. The You Owe Me app is available for iPhone only. There is no Android app or full browser version of the app. On Android, someone can still use the website’s free browser tools or open a read-only Live Link shared by a person using the iPhone app. Creating or editing an ongoing You Owe Me record requires the iPhone app, and Balance Sync is not available to an Android participant.

### Is YouOweMe only for loans?

No. It can track informal loans, IOUs, shared expenses, temporary support, family reimbursements, roommate bills, partner spending, client balances, recurring costs, and repayments over time.

### Can it help me ask someone to pay me back politely?

Yes. Money Conversations can generate a follow-up message from the real balance and history, with a tone that fits the relationship.

### Does the other person need the app?

Not always. Use Live Link when one person keeps the record and the other person only needs an always-current browser view without installing the app. Use Balance Sync when exactly two You Owe Me users both want supported connected entries to stay updated across their phones.

### Can I use it for family reimbursements?

Yes. You can track purchases, bills, subscriptions, recurring charges, and repayments for parents, siblings, children, or other family members. If the main job is paying for an elderly parent, start with the elderly parent expense tracker.

### Can I split bills and shared expenses?

Yes. You can split expenses equally or with custom amounts, then keep the result in a running balance instead of recalculating every time.

### What languages is the You Owe Me app available in?

The You Owe Me app interface is localized in seven languages: English, Arabic, French, German, Japanese, Portuguese, and Spanish. This applies to the app; the You Owe Me website is currently available in English only.

### Does it work offline?

Yes. YouOweMe can be used without mandatory sign-up and supports offline tracking.

### Repayment plans that stay current

Add an optional plan to any active loan when “pay it back later” needs a clearer path. Choose weekly, every-two-weeks, or monthly payments and set the first payment date.

Start from the decision that is easiest to make: compare lower-payment, balanced, and faster-payoff options; enter a regular payment amount; choose a projected payoff date; or choose the number of payments. The app calculates the rest before you save.

The plan uses the same balance and payment history as the loan. If a payment is smaller than planned, the app shows what is still needed for that installment. If it is larger, the extra amount can move the plan ahead. Additional borrowing, corrections, or deleted entries update the remaining schedule, projected payoff, and next reminder.

See what is due next and whether the plan is on track, ahead, due today, or overdue. Interest can be left out, added as one agreed fixed amount, or calculated on the declining unpaid balance.

If circumstances change, start a revised plan from the current balance while keeping the earlier plan in history. Ending a plan stops future schedule tracking and its reminder without deleting the loan or its payments. One active repayment plan is included for free; paid access supports unlimited active plans.

## Balance Sync FAQ

### Do both people need You Owe Me for Balance Sync?

Yes. Balance Sync connects exactly two You Owe Me users. Both participants need full Sign in with Apple accounts and a compatible version of the app, currently You Owe Me 6.9.4 or later. If only one person wants the app, use Live Link or another shareable summary instead.

### How is Balance Sync different from Live Link?

Live Link is the one-way option: one person maintains the record while the other person gets an always-current, read-only browser view without installing the app. Balance Sync is the two-way option: exactly two You Owe Me users can both update supported connected entries from their own phones.

### Can Live Link and Balance Sync be active for the same connected person?

Not at the same time. Use Live Link when one person maintains the record and the other person only needs browser visibility. Use Balance Sync when exactly two app users both need to update supported entries.

### How does someone connect with Balance Sync?

The sender opens a person, chooses Share and then Balance Sync, reviews what will be shared, and creates a private invitation by QR code or link. The invitation expires after seven days and should only be sent to the intended person. The recipient signs in, reviews the invitation and data disclosure, and accepts the balance into a new, separate person record.

### Does Balance Sync update instantly?

Do not treat it as instantaneous or real-time. Synchronization runs after local changes, when the app becomes active, when connectivity returns, and when someone manually refreshes or retries.

### What information synchronizes?

Balance Sync synchronizes the supported information needed to reproduce connected balance entries, including amount, debt direction, currency, date, note or reason, entry icon, archived status, and supported interest or linked balance-record information.

### What stays private on each phone?

The local name assigned to the person, profile images, entry images and attachments, reminders, recurrence rules, and other local-only settings are not copied to the other user.

### Does Balance Sync upload every old transaction?

No. It shares the history needed to reproduce the current balance accurately—normally entries since the most recent point when the two people were even. Older settled history usually stays only on the original device. If the app cannot reproduce the balance exactly, setup is rejected rather than creating an incorrect connection.

### What happens if both people edit the same entry?

Only that entry pauses. The user explicitly chooses which complete version to keep, preventing a silent overwrite. Other connected activity can continue normally.

### Does Balance Sync work with Group Paybacks?

Not currently. Balance Sync is for a supported balance between exactly two people. Group Payback activity does not synchronize.

### What happens when someone disconnects?

Future synchronization stops for both people and the same connection cannot be resumed. Entries already copied to each phone remain as ordinary local data, so disconnecting does not erase either person’s local history.

### How many Balance Sync connections are included?

The free plan supports one active or pending Balance Sync connection. Paid access removes the app-level connection limit. Existing connections continue working even when the user cannot create another one.

A note can say “Maya owes $30.” It usually does not explain whether Maya paid $20 later, whether the remaining $10 was covered by another shared cost, or whether you already followed up.

### Chat shows conversation, not the current balance

Messages are useful for context, but they are not designed to keep a balance visible after normal conversation continues.

### Spreadsheets track numbers, but not the next conversation

A spreadsheet can be accurate if you maintain it. It usually does not help you decide when to follow up, what to say, or what context the other person already saw.

### Payment history proves movement, not meaning

A transfer can show that money moved. It may not show what it covered, whether it was partial, or what remains open.

### Calculators solve the moment, not the relationship

A calculator gives an answer now. It does not keep the answer updated after the next expense, repayment, reminder, or receipt.

### Group ledgers are powerful, but not always necessary

A collaborative expense app can be right for a trip or shared house. It may be too much when one person just wants a clear record and a way to share the balance when needed.

Track what a client owes, what they already paid, and what is still open — without turning a simple payment record into a full accounting system.

## What is a client payment record?

A client payment record is a simple history of what a client was charged, what they paid, when they paid it, and what balance is still open. It can include deposits, partial payments, cash payments, repeat service charges, notes, reminders, and receipt-style confirmations.

If an extra amount has already been agreed, YouOweMe can record it as percentage interest that grows over time or as one fixed interest amount added once. It keeps the private balance clear; it is not a replacement for formal invoices, contracts, accounting, tax, or legal advice.

If the situation is one original client amount with deposits or partial payments, the Partial Repayment Calculator can calculate what remains before you create a longer client record.

For one invoice or one payment, a message or note may be enough. But when payments happen in steps, work repeats, or the balance stays open, a dedicated record helps prevent confusion later.

## Why client payments get hard to track

Most client payment confusion does not come from bad intentions. It comes from small details living in different places: a text message, a bank transfer, a cash payment, a note, a receipt, and your memory.

If you only need to reconstruct the current amount from a few charges and payments, start with the Running Balance Calculator.

### Deposits and final payments split the balance

A client pays part now and the rest later. A few weeks later, it is easy to forget the exact remaining amount.

### Repeat work creates a running balance

Lessons, sessions, service visits, or recurring work can keep adding new amounts before the old balance is fully paid.

### Cash and transfers do not explain the context

A payment may show that money arrived, but not what it covered or what still remains.

### Messages get buried

Chats are useful for conversation, but they rarely give one clean answer to “what is still open?”

### Spreadsheets require discipline

A spreadsheet can work, but it is easy to forget updates when you are moving between clients, messages, and real work.

### Follow-ups are easier when the record is clear

A factual balance makes a payment reminder calmer and less personal: here is what was charged, what was paid, and what remains.

## Simple payment confirmation messages

Use factual, calm wording when you need to confirm a deposit, partial payment, balance summary, follow-up, or paid-in-full status.

If you need to calculate the remaining balance before confirming the payment, use the Partial Repayment Calculator first.

If the client already paid and you only need a confirmation message, use the Repayment Receipt Generator.

### Deposit received

Thanks, I’ve marked the $200 deposit for [project/service]. That leaves $400 open, with the remaining amount due after [next step/date].

### Partial payment

Thanks, I received $150 today and marked it against [project/service]. The remaining balance is $250.

### Balance summary

Here is the current payment summary: [amount charged], [amount paid], [amount remaining]. I’m keeping the record updated so we both have the same numbers.

### Payment follow-up

Quick payment check-in: I have [service/project] recorded as [total amount], with [paid amount] already paid and [remaining amount] still open. Does that match your records?

### Paid in full

Thanks, I’ve marked the balance as paid in full for [project/service]. I have the final payment recorded on [date].
