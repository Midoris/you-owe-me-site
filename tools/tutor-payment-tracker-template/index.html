What each Excel entry includes
Date
The date the charge applies or the payment was received.
Account ID
The payer account from Accounts, such as A001. Use the uppercase ID exactly as shown so people with similar names do not get mixed together.
Student / context
The student or lesson context, especially when one household pays for several students.
Description
What the charge or payment relates to.
Type and amount
Choose Charge or Payment, then enter a positive amount. Use a separate row for each event.
Reference / note
A payment reference or short note that helps you recognize the entry later.
Check

You Owe Me does not receive the data you enter in these files. Your spreadsheet app or cloud provider handles any copy you upload or share. A workbook may contain several families' details, so share only the relevant payer's record—not the whole file by default.

Track money owed, not just whether a lesson is paid

A single paid/unpaid checkbox is easy to outgrow. One payment may cover several lessons, and a partial payment may leave only part of the total outstanding. Record the agreed charges and actual payments separately, then calculate the balance for the person responsible for paying.

Who is paying?

Use one account for each payer or household. If a parent pays for two children, use the student/context field to identify the lessons while keeping the family's money balance together.

What was charged?

Add a charge for a lesson or other amount you have actually agreed is payable. A future calendar booking does not become a charge automatically.

What was received?

Add a payment only when you receive it. Keep the date and a useful reference, whether it arrived as cash or a transfer.

Set it up in four steps

  1. Add the people responsible for payment

    On Accounts, enter each payer or household beside a preset Account ID. Keep those IDs unchanged. Choose one currency for the file. The period label is for your reference; it does not filter entries by date.

  2. Bring forward an earlier balance once

    If a payer already owes an agreed amount from an earlier period, enter it as the opening balance. Leave it blank or use zero when nothing is outstanding. Do not also enter the same earlier charges in Entries.

  3. Record charges and received payments

    On Entries, use a separate row for each charge or payment. Choose the right Account ID and Type, enter a positive amount, and add enough context to recognize it later. The workbook includes every filled entry row, regardless of row order or the period label.

  4. Review before following up

    Fix any Check messages first, then compare the account summary with your actual records. If a balance is negative, review the payment or overpayment; the workbook does not convert it into prepaid lesson credits. Start a fresh file for a new period when needed, carrying the checked closing balance forward once.

The prepared ranges cover 30 accounts and 200 entries. Rows added outside those ranges are not included automatically. Keep a copy of each completed period so you retain the history.

Questions about tracking tutoring payments

What should a tutor payment tracker include?

Record the payer, student or lesson context, date, agreed charge, actual payment and a useful reference. Keep charges and payments separate so partial payments leave a clear remaining balance.

Can one parent pay for more than one student?

Yes. Use one payer account for the household and identify the student in each entry's context field. Use separate accounts when different people are responsible for separate balances.

How do I record a payment covering several lessons?

Record the amount received once as a Payment entry for that payer. Add a note identifying the lessons or period it covers. Do not repeat the full payment on each lesson row.

Can I carry an unpaid balance into the next month?

Yes. Enter the checked closing amount once as the new file's opening balance. Do not also copy the same old charges or payments into the new period. Keep the previous file for the detailed history.

Does the tracker manage prepaid lesson packages?

No. It tracks money amounts, not remaining lesson units or attendance. A negative balance is flagged for review; it is not converted into a lesson-credit total.

Can I use the file in Google Sheets or Apple Numbers?

You can import the Excel or CSV file into another spreadsheet app, but formatting, validation and formulas may behave differently. Check the Excel balances against the worked example after importing. The CSV is an entry log without formulas or summaries.

Does the tracker create invoices or take payments?

No. The workbook records amounts you enter. The app can create payment-record summaries, but it is not a formal invoicing system and does not process payments.

Do students or parents need to install You Owe Me?

No. A tutor can keep their own records in the app without asking the payer to install it. The spreadsheet can also be used independently of the app.
