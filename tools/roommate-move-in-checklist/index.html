How to fill in the planner columns
Room and item
Where it belongs and what it is. Add a quantity or size to the item name when that matters.
Decision
Bring, Buy, Skip or Undecided.
Owner or buyer
For Bring, name the existing owner. For Buy, name the person arranging the purchase.
Expected cost and actual cost
Keep the estimate separate from what was actually paid. Leave an unknown amount blank.
Currency
Write the currency next to the amounts. Do not add different currencies together.
Agreed shares
Name the people contributing and their agreed amounts or percentages. Do not assume every roommate is included.
Status and needed by
Use Not started, Bringing, Ordered, Ready or Skipped, and add a date if useful.
Move-out agreement and notes
Write what you agreed about keeping, selling or discussing the item when someone leaves. Add condition, receipt location or any open question.

Start with three separate lists

For every item, decide whether someone is bringing it, you need to buy it, or you can skip it for now. Record who owns existing belongings. For a new shared purchase, agree on the budget, the payer and each person's share before spending.

Already owned

Write down who is bringing the item and who owns it. Sharing the use of a table does not automatically mean sharing its ownership or paying its owner.

Still needed

Choose a buyer, set an expected cost and agree who will contribute. Leave the actual cost empty until the purchase happens.

Bought and agreed

Record the actual amount paid and the shares everyone accepted. Track repayments separately so a planned purchase never becomes a debt by accident.

Room-by-room starter checklist

Check what the home already includes before buying anything. This is a starting list, not a requirement to purchase every item.

Kitchen

  • Plates, bowls and mugs
  • Cutlery and cooking utensils
  • Pots and pans
  • Kettle or coffee maker
  • Food containers
  • Dish soap and towels

Living and dining

  • Sofa or chairs
  • Table and chairs
  • Lamps
  • Rugs or curtains

Bathroom

  • Shower curtain or bath mat
  • Toilet brush
  • Hand soap
  • Shared cleaning supplies

Cleaning and laundry

  • Vacuum or broom
  • Mop and bucket
  • Drying rack
  • Laundry supplies

Shared essentials

  • Internet router, if needed
  • Bins and bin bags
  • Basic tools
  • Extension leads, if suitable

Personal items

  • Bed and bedding
  • Desk or personal furniture
  • Personal towels
  • Personal toiletries

Keep personal purchases separate unless you explicitly agree to share their cost. Check the lease or ask the landlord which furnishings and equipment are included.

Agree on these points before a shared purchase

  1. Who is using the item? Include only the people who agreed to contribute.
  2. What is the spending limit? Confirm whether delivery or other purchase costs are included.
  3. Who pays first? Write down who will make the purchase and how the others will repay them.
  4. What are the shares? Agree on equal shares, specific amounts or percentages before buying.
  5. What happens at move-out? Record the arrangement you all accept, and revisit it if circumstances change.

A short written note can help everyone remember the same decision. This planner is an organizational record, not a legal agreement or a valuation tool.

Questions about moving in with roommates

What should a roommate move-in spreadsheet include?

List the item, room, whether to bring or buy it, the owner or buyer, expected and actual costs, currency, agreed shares, purchase status and any move-out agreement. Keep planned purchases separate from amounts actually owed.

Should we split the cost of furniture someone already owns?

Do not assume a contribution is owed just because the household will use the item. Discuss whether the owner is lending it for shared use, selling a share or making another arrangement, and record only what everyone actually agrees.

Does this spreadsheet calculate who owes whom?

No. It is a move-in inventory and purchase planner. Use the separate roommate expense tracker for shared expense calculations and repayments, or You Owe Me to keep ongoing money records on iPhone and iPad.

Can I use the planner in Google Sheets?

Yes. Import the Excel or CSV file into your own Google Sheets document and check the layout. The CSV contains the starter list and planning columns; the Excel file also includes a worked example.

What happens when a roommate moves out?

Review the ownership and move-out notes together. Agree what will be kept, taken or sold, and record any new amount to repay only after everyone involved accepts it. The planner does not calculate a buyout or determine legal ownership.

Do all roommates need the app?

No. You can share the spreadsheet using your usual tools. If one person chooses You Owe Me to track repayments, the other roommates do not need to install it for that person to keep records.
