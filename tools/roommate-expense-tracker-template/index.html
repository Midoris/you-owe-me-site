Inside the workbook

What the workbook keeps separate

A trustworthy roommate record separates what happened from what happened later. Expenses show what was paid and how it was shared. Repayments show money sent between roommates. The summary combines both without erasing either history.

Agreed shares, not invented fairness

Use equal shares only when everyone included in that cost agreed to an equal split. Use custom shares for an unequal arrangement that has already been discussed.

Repayments stay visible

A repayment changes the open position, but it does not change who originally paid a bill or how that bill was shared.

One verified carry-forward

Bring a prior closing position into the next period once. Do not carry the same balance forward and record it again as a new expense.

Five-minute setup

Set up the spreadsheet in five minutes

Start on the Setup sheet, then enter each activity only in the input columns. Leave formula cells and summary cells unchanged.

  1. Add the household and period

    Enter an optional household name, the month or date range you are reviewing, one currency, and two to six unique roommate names.

  2. Enter an opening position only when one exists

    Use the closing positions from the last verified period. A positive opening position means that roommate should receive money; a negative position means they owe money. All opening positions must add up to zero.

  3. Record each expense once

    Add the date, expense, category, amount, and the roommate who paid. Do not add a second row just because the cost is being repaid later.

  4. Confirm who shared the cost

    For an equal split, include only the roommates who shared that expense. For a custom split, enter the agreed amount assigned to each included roommate and check that the shares equal the full expense.

  5. Add repayments and review the summary

    Record each transfer on the Repayments sheet, then review the Summary sheet together before anyone sends the final settlement.

Tip: if a cost is disputed, leave it outside the accepted balance until the roommates clarify whether it belongs and how it should be shared.

Before anyone settles

Review the month before anyone pays

A correct formula is not enough if the inputs are unclear. Review the record together while the expenses are still easy to recognize.

Monthly review checklist

  • Every expense has a date, description, amount, and payer.
  • Only the roommates who shared each cost are included.
  • Custom shares add up to the full expense amount.
  • Each repayment appears once and points from the sender to the recipient.
  • Opening positions came from one previously verified closing summary.
  • Current positions add up to zero before using the settlement suggestions.
  • Disputed items are labeled and kept outside the accepted balance.

A calm message to send with the summary

Here’s the roommate expense summary for this month. It includes the bills listed in the sheet, the shares we agreed on, and repayments already sent. Please check anything that looks different from your record before we settle it.

After someone pays, use the repayment receipt generator to confirm what was paid and whether anything remains.

A shared summary is an invitation to check the record—not a demand, collection notice, or proof that a disputed amount is owed.

Questions and boundaries

Roommate spreadsheet questions

What is a roommate expense tracker spreadsheet?

A roommate expense tracker spreadsheet is a reusable record of shared household costs, who paid, each roommate’s agreed share, repayments already sent, and the balance still open.

Does the template support unequal shares?

Yes. Use custom shares when roommates have already agreed that rent, utilities, or another cost should not be divided equally. The assigned shares for one expense should add up to the full expense amount.

Can one expense exclude a roommate?

Yes. Include only the roommates who shared that cost. A personal purchase or a bill used by only part of the household should not automatically be assigned to everyone.

How should roommate repayments be recorded?

Record a repayment separately from the original expense. Include who paid whom, the date, and the amount so the workbook can reduce the open balance without changing the expense history.

Can a balance carry into the next month?

Yes. Enter the agreed closing position from the previous period once as the next period’s opening position. Do not subtract the same repayment or balance a second time.

Does the website receive my spreadsheet data?

No. The download happens from this website, but the information you enter stays in the file unless you choose to upload or share that file elsewhere.

Can I open the workbook in Apple Numbers or Google Sheets?

The .xlsx workbook is designed for Microsoft Excel and uses standard spreadsheet formulas. It can also be opened in Apple Numbers or uploaded to Google Sheets, but formatting, dropdowns, or formulas may behave differently. Check the Summary sheet after importing before relying on the result.

Important boundaries

This template is for informal household organization. It does not decide fair rent, create or prove a legal debt, replace a lease or roommate agreement, process payments, collect money, provide accounting records, or give legal or tax advice. Record only costs and shares the people involved understand and accept. Keep disputed items outside the accepted balance until they are clarified.
