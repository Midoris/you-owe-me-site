Open the loan importer

Start with one loan and the events that actually happened

You do not need a perfect spreadsheet. You do need enough detail to tell the original lending, later advances and actual repayments apart.

Who and which direction

Name the other person and say whether you lent the money or borrowed it. Keep different people's loans separate.

Dates, amounts and currency

Include the date and amount of each event and identify the currency. A number such as “100” is not enough if its meaning is unclear.

Lending and repayments

Label money lent separately from money repaid. A further advance increases what is owed; an actual repayment reduces it.

Plans versus history

Keep promised future payments separate from payments that have already happened. A due date or plan is not proof of a repayment.

If the source mixes several loans, make a copy containing just the one you want to bring across. Keep your original file so you can compare it with the review.

Choose the source you already have

Use the format that keeps your history clearest. You do not need to convert everything to Excel first.

Excel or CSV

Use a simple table with one event per row. Include dates, descriptions and amounts, and identify which rows are lending and which are repayments. Excel files must use .xlsx; the older .xls format is not supported.

Pasted notes or a copied table

Paste the relevant text and explain who lent to whom. Keep currency and dates with the amounts. If a copied table loses its columns, add short labels so each amount still makes sense.

A Word document

Use a .docx document containing the relevant loan record. Remove unrelated loans from the copy you submit. PDF and older .doc files are not supported by this document input.

A photo

Use a clear image of the relevant record, with readable dates and amounts. Handwriting can be interpreted, but check the result carefully. Crop out unrelated personal information and make sure the loan direction is clear.

The current browser intake accepts documents up to 2 MB and one image up to 20 MB. Other processing limits can apply; the importer shows any issue it needs you to resolve.

Review the history, then continue on your iPhone

1. Open the importer and add your source

Start from the loan importer, then paste your notes or choose the supported document or photo. Using interpretation sends that source content to You Owe Me's service and OpenAI. If you prefer not to use AI interpretation, choose manual entry instead.

Start with your recordRead how import data is handled

2. Check what the review understood

Compare the result with your source before continuing. Check the person, whether you lent or borrowed, the currency, the original amount and each dated event. Correct anything that was misunderstood and resolve the questions the review identifies.

  • The initial loan is counted once.
  • Further amounts lent are separate from repayments.
  • Every actual repayment appears once, with the right date and amount.
  • Promised payments have not been counted as received.
  • The resulting balance agrees with the history you can confirm.

For the example above, check for $1,300 lent, $450 repaid and $850 outstanding. If a review differs, inspect the events rather than changing the source to force a particular total.

3. Keep any future plan separate

You can bring across the history without adding a future repayment schedule. Where the review offers a supported interest-free plan, check its amount, frequency and dates separately. Do not turn missing or uncertain terms into an invented agreement.

An import does not reproduce every lender's interest calculation, fees or allocation rules. Keep formal lender statements as your reference for those debts.

4. Use the reviewed loan's continuation link

After review, the import flow can prepare a private link or QR code for that loan. Open it on your iPhone and follow the App Clip or full-app continuation. Finish the review and saving steps in the app; opening a link alone is not the same as saving the loan.

The two QR codes have different jobs

The private continuation QR carries your reviewed loan draft. The App Store QR on this guide opens the app's download page; it does not carry your loan history. Use the continuation offered by the importer when you want to bring the draft across.

The private transfer expires after 24 hours. Treat its link, QR code and any recovery file as private: someone who has them can read the draft.

5. Record the next repayment in the same loan

Once the new loan has been saved, continue its record in You Owe Me. In our example, a later $100 repayment would reduce the outstanding amount from $850 to $750. Record it when it actually happens; do not import the same history again to add each new payment.

If the result does not look right

Pause before continuing. These are checks to make against your source, not reasons to accept an uncertain result.

The balance is too high or too low

Look for a missing or duplicated repayment, a further advance treated as a repayment, or an original loan counted twice. Compare the individual events before the final total.

The direction is reversed

Make it explicit who lent and who borrowed. “Paid Jordan 100” could mean lending, repayment or an unrelated expense without that context.

A date or amount is unclear

Correct the source or resolve the review question using information you can confirm. Do not guess just to finish the import.

The source contains several loans

Separate them into one-loan records and review each import individually. This flow is not a full account or group migration.

You can use manual entry if a source is too ambiguous to interpret reliably. Keep the original record while you check the new loan.

What this import does—and what it does not

A reviewed new loan

This flow creates one new loan after review and saving. It does not merge history into an existing loan, keep a spreadsheet continuously synchronized or automatically import the manual calculator rows on the website.

Source processing and private continuation

Interpretation sends the submitted source to You Owe Me's service and OpenAI. Manual entry avoids that interpretation step. Preparing a private continuation creates a temporary transfer; its 24-hour expiry is separate from provider processing and retention. Read the loan-import privacy details before using personal information.

App capacity and supported terms

Saving and optional planning are subject to the app's current capacity and plan limits. Import is not a promise of unlimited loans, every interest method, payment collection or an automatically verified bank record.

Questions about importing a loan

Can I import a loan from Excel?

Yes. The browser intake accepts .xlsx files, as well as CSV, DOCX, pasted text and a photo. Prepare one loan, identify lending and repayments clearly, and review the interpreted details before saving.

Can I import a handwritten loan record?

You can submit a clear photo, including handwriting. Readability and context affect interpretation, so check the dates, amounts, people and repayment direction in the review. Manual entry is available if the source is unclear.

Do I need a fixed repayment schedule?

No. You can keep the actual history without a future schedule. If you add a supported plan, review it separately from payments that have already happened.

Can I add the imported history to an existing loan?

This import creates one new reviewed loan. It does not merge into an existing loan. After saving, record later payments in that loan rather than importing the same history again.

Will changes in my spreadsheet update the app?

No. Import is a reviewed transfer of one loan's history, not continuous synchronization with Excel, Google Sheets or another file.

Can I transfer the rows from the manual website tracker automatically?

The manual website tracker and the source-import flow are separate. Its calculator rows do not automatically become an imported loan. Use a supported source or enter the history in the import review.

Does scanning the App Store QR transfer my loan?

No. The App Store QR opens the download page. To bring a reviewed draft across, use the private continuation link or QR provided by the importer and finish the app's saving steps.

Can I start directly in the app?

Yes. You can use New loan → Import loan history from Records or the other person's screen. Review the result and the selected storage or profile before saving.

Is my source sent for AI interpretation?

If you use interpretation, the submitted content goes to You Owe Me's service and OpenAI. Manual entry avoids AI interpretation. Private continuation has separate transfer handling; see the loan-import privacy details for the full explanation.
