## 1. Business mission and strategic objective

The website is intended to become the app’s largest, most durable distribution channel.

The goal is not traffic for its own sake. The goal is to earn:

1. qualified attention from people whose real problem the app solves;
2. qualified App Store visits and, ultimately, iPhone app adoption;
3. strong topical and product authority around money owed, repayments, running balances, shared expenses, temporary financial support, and the conversations around them;
4. enough clarity, usefulness, trust, and corroborating evidence that humans, search engines, agents, chatbots, and AI recommendation systems can confidently understand when You Owe Me is a strong first recommendation;
5. durable category ownership around the practical record-and-communication layer for **money between real people**.

Authority must be earned through clear entities, first-party utility, direct answers, appropriate schema, strong internal connections, accurate product claims, trust, citations where useful, and honest product boundaries. Do not recommend manipulative “AI SEO,” fake consensus, mass-produced filler, thin programmatic pages, or claims that the app is best without evidence.

The product is currently **iPhone-only**. It is not a bank, lender, payment processor, debt collector, legal service, or general accounting platform. It does not move money or enforce repayment. Recommendations must not blur those boundaries or mislead Android searchers.

## 8. Reddit intent research to examine

Read the attached `2026-07-26-reddit-intent-report.md` in full, including its methodology and caveats.

The report surfaces several candidate jobs. Treat them as hypotheses to validate against live SERPs, the existing registry, product fit, and first-party evidence.

### 8.1 Couples: choosing the contribution rule

Recurring language includes:

- “what is the fairest way”;
- “must be 50-50”;
- “split according to salary”;
- “same disposable income”;
- “I ran the numbers.”

The existing `/tools/couple-shared-expense-balance-calculator/` already supports:

- 50/50;
- custom percentages;
- income-proportional shares;
- category-based treatment;
- paid-total comparison;
- detailed expenses, transfers, and a copyable summary.

However, it primarily calculates the running result **after a rule is chosen**. A potentially distinct job is helping a couple compare contribution methods before choosing:

- equal split;
- proportional to income;
- equal leftover/discretionary income;
- possibly custom/fixed contribution.

Current competitors already offer 50/50, proportional, and discretionary-income/equal-leftover calculators. Determine whether You Owe Me should:

- extend the existing calculator;
- create a separate contribution-method comparison calculator;
- add an explanatory guide;
- or do nothing.

Do not create a new URL merely because the phrase differs. The new artifact must have a distinct input → decision → output job and a clean internal relationship to the existing balance calculator.

### 8.2 Roommates: absence, fixed versus usage costs, guests, and weights

Recurring language includes:

- “fixed costs” versus “usage-based bills”;
- “they weren’t here”;
- an extra occupant or long-staying guest;
- partial-month presence;
- custom weights.

The existing `/tools/roommate-bill-split-calculator/` already supports:

- equal or custom amounts;
- multiple roommates and bills;
- which roommates are included in each bill;
- repayments and prior balance;
- a copyable message.

It does not currently appear to model:

- days present or away;
- fixed versus usage-based portions;
- a temporary extra occupant;
- occupancy/usage weights.

Research whether the strongest move is a focused expansion of the existing calculator, a new utility-bill-specific tool, a supporting guide, or some combination. Prefer an existing-page expansion when it satisfies the same core job without making the tool confusing.

### 8.3 Tickets and event payback

Recurring language includes:

- “one person books”;
- “I’m usually buying tickets”;
- “pay by [date]”;
- uncertainty about whether the ticket is reserved, transferred, resold, refunded, or still unpaid.

The site already covers tickets inside:

- group payback tracking;
- the group payback calculator;
- group reminder guidance;
- cancellation/refund/replacement guidance.

A ticket page is justified only if it owns a distinct lifecycle that existing pages do not:

1. agreement before purchase;
2. who currently controls the ticket;
3. payment deadline before the event;
4. transfer state;
5. refund/resale/replacement if plans change;
6. final balance and message.

Validate whether real search behavior supports a dedicated guide or tool. Reject it if it would be a thin “tickets” restatement of the group cluster.
