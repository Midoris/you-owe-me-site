# Ten years of You Owe Me: strategic page plan

Planning date: 24 August 2026
Proposed permanent route: `/10-years/`
Working page type: permanent product-history and trust page with a removable anniversary-campaign module
Status: strategic content plan only; not website copy, a design specification, or an implementation prompt

Evidence base: [10-year anniversary research sourcebook](./10-year-anniversary-sourcebook.md)

## Strategic conclusion

**Proceed with this standalone page.**

The page is justified, but only in a specific form. It should not be a generic birthday announcement, a sale landing page, an exhaustive changelog, or a second Features page. Its permanent job is to answer a high-value trust question that the current website leaves distributed across several pages:

> Is You Owe Me an established, actively maintained product that I can trust for records I may need for months or years?

The best answer is a source-backed story of continuity: the app began with a simple running record in 2016; it now handles much more complex situations, including separate Loan Records and adaptive Repayment Plans; and the purpose has remained the same—keep money between real people clear.

This is primarily a branded trust, product-positioning, retention, referral, and campaign-destination page. It may earn branded search visibility and become a useful source for AI answers, journalists, reviewers, and prospective customers, but it should not be justified as a high-volume non-branded SEO page. That would force it toward loan-tracker keywords already served better elsewhere.

The recommended content model is:

> A permanent ten-year story, organized around “what changed / what never changed,” supported by a concise evidence-led timeline, one flagship product-evolution example, carefully selected customer proof, and a short practical clarity framework. During the ten-day campaign, a separate removable module connects this story to the native anniversary experience, App Store event, and anniversary offer.

### Why this page passes despite modest search volume

The page does not need to win a large generic keyword to be valuable. Its distinct site job is due diligence. People considering an ongoing money record care whether the product will remain usable, maintained, and understandable. A decade of platform adaptation and current development is meaningful evidence at precisely that decision point.

Its likely discovery paths are:

- The homepage or a temporary anniversary notice.
- The App Store in-app event or a shared anniversary link.
- The Reviews page while evaluating legitimacy.
- The Features, Quick Start, or Privacy & Data pages while evaluating fit.
- Branded queries about You Owe Me, its history, age, maintenance, or anniversary.
- AI answers or editorial references that need an authoritative launch date and concise product chronology.
- Existing customers sharing or revisiting the milestone.

## 2. Page promise

This page should help a visitor decide whether You Owe Me is a serious, durable, actively improving product—and understand why ten years of additions have not changed its original purpose. It should provide the website's authoritative 2016–2026 product story, show how a simple running balance matured into clearer ways to handle loans, repayment plans, shared costs, reminders, communication, and records, and extract a few practical principles anyone can use to keep money between people clearer. A visitor who never downloads the app should still leave with a more useful way to judge and maintain a personal money record.

### Search and editorial framing

- **Primary intent:** branded product history, longevity, and maintenance trust.
- **Secondary intent:** anniversary discovery, current-product evaluation, and authoritative entity facts for search and answer systems.
- **Working title:** `10 Years of You Owe Me (2016–2026) | Our Story`
- **Working meta description:** `You Owe Me launched in 2016 with a simple goal: keep money between people clear. See how the app grew from running IOUs to loans, repayment plans, reminders, sharing, and more.`
- **Do not target:** generic `10 year anniversary` traffic, broad `loan tracker app` rankings, `best IOU app`, or `is it legit` as the primary phrase. Those intents either attract irrelevant visitors or belong to established solution and review pages.

## 3. Reader state

There is not one anniversary reader. The page needs to serve four closely related states without turning into four pages.

### A prospective customer evaluating trust

- **Feels:** cautious, curious, and possibly surprised that a focused app has existed for ten years.
- **Fears:** trusting important long-running records to an abandoned, fragile, or overly promotional app; losing history; finding that a simple-looking app cannot handle a real loan.
- **Already knows:** the app may track IOUs or loans, but not whether it is established or actively maintained.
- **Wants next:** concise evidence, a view of the present product, customer proof, and a calm route to Features, Privacy & Data, Reviews, or the App Store.

### An existing paid customer

- **Feels:** recognition, pride, or quiet curiosity about how the product reached this point.
- **Fears:** that a celebration will become another sales interruption or ignore their support.
- **Already knows:** the product's practical value, often from long-term use.
- **Wants next:** a genuine thank-you, a meaningful retrospective, and confidence that the app remains alive.

### An existing free or returning user

- **Feels:** curiosity about the anniversary and possibly interest in the offer.
- **Fears:** a thin sales page or pressure to upgrade before understanding what is being celebrated.
- **Already knows:** at least the core balance model, though perhaps not the recent expansion.
- **Wants next:** see what changed, open the native anniversary experience, and optionally inspect the offer.

### A reviewer, search system, or AI answer engine

- **Needs:** unambiguous facts, dates, eras, feature names, product boundaries, and links to deeper evidence.
- **Does not need:** vague claims such as “thriving,” a wall of release numbers, or campaign language mixed into permanent facts.
- **Wants next:** a quotable launch date, a concise chronology, and a clear description of what the product is and is not.

### Emotional sequence the page should create

1. **Recognition:** this is genuinely ten years old.
2. **Reassurance:** it has been maintained and adapted, not merely left in the store.
3. **Understanding:** its increasing depth serves the same original job.
4. **Human connection:** real people have relied on it, and their support mattered.
5. **Agency:** choose whether to explore the history, current product, privacy, reviews, app, or campaign experience.

## 4. First-screen promise

### Recommended first-screen copy

**Eyebrow:** `2016–2026`

**Headline:**

> Ten years of clearer money between people.

**Opening:**

> You Owe Me launched on August 26, 2016 with one focused idea: when money passes between people, the record should stay clear. Ten years later, the app can handle everything from everyday IOUs to separate loans and repayment plans—but that idea has never changed.

**Primary first-screen action:** `See the ten-year journey`

This should move to the history artifact, not leave the page.

**Optional secondary campaign action, shown only during the event:** `Open the 10-year experience`

The App Store badge or download CTA should not dominate the first screen. The visitor's first question is “why does this milestone matter?”, not “where is the purchase button?”

### Why this promise works

- It states the authoritative launch date and duration without corporate language.
- It introduces the product through a durable human problem, not a feature list.
- It makes the advanced loan capability evidence of evolution rather than a keyword insertion.
- It avoids unsupported audience-growth claims.
- It works before, during, and after the anniversary campaign.

### First-screen alternatives to reject

- `You Owe Me turns 10!` — true but says nothing about why a visitor should care.
- `10 days only` — turns the milestone into a promotion and ages immediately.
- `A decade of innovation` — generic and difficult to prove.
- `The best IOU app for 10 years` — unverifiable and too close to acquisition copy.
- `Ten years old. More useful than ever.` — usable as a supporting line, but too self-evaluative for the primary promise.

## 5. Content strategy options

### Option A: A chronological product documentary

**Emphasis:** selected milestones from 2016 to 2026, with historical context and product screenshots.

**Practical value:** gives a unique first-party history and lets visitors verify longevity and continuity.

**Product fit:** high. Ten years of maintenance matters for an app that holds ongoing personal money records.

**Risk:** a conventional timeline can become inward-looking, technical, and long. It may reward existing customers but fail to explain what the history means to a new visitor.

### Option B: A thank-you and customer-story page

**Emphasis:** gratitude, selected reviews, use cases, and a short note from the creator.

**Practical value:** warmth and recognition for existing users; strong sharing potential during the campaign.

**Product fit:** medium to high. The app exists in human relationships, so customer voice belongs naturally.

**Risk:** without original historical structure, it becomes a thin marketing page and duplicates Reviews. It can also make paid customers feel that the thank-you is a pretext for an offer.

### Option C: A “then and now” product-evolution page

**Emphasis:** the 2016 running-balance core compared with the 2026 product, led by Loan Records and Repayment Plans.

**Practical value:** quickly demonstrates depth, current relevance, and why the app has become more capable.

**Product fit:** very high. Loans and plans are the clearest modern proof of purposeful evolution.

**Risk:** it can become another Features page or overstate how long recent features have existed. A large capability matrix would be promotional and invite cannibalization.

### Option D: A public release and engineering dossier

**Emphasis:** 124 App Store version records, release cadence, technical migrations, archives, and repository history.

**Practical value:** unusually strong evidence that the project is real and actively maintained.

**Product fit:** medium. It answers seriousness, but not the human outcome.

**Risk:** technical vanity metrics can confuse builds, archives, and public releases; they expose private operational detail; and most visitors do not care about commit counts. It would feel defensive rather than confident.

### Recommended option: the continuity-proof story

Use Option A as the backbone, Option C as the clearest product proof, and Option B as the emotional close. Keep Option D as internal substantiation, with at most one carefully translated public fact.

The governing editorial device should be:

> What people needed → what the app added → what never changed.

This makes the chronology useful. It does not merely say that features shipped; it shows how the product kept solving the same category of misunderstanding at greater depth.

External calibration supports this choice. Doist's public history works because its condensed timeline is anchored to a continuing mission, while Messenger's tenth-birthday page uses gratitude plus a feature bundle—a useful campaign pattern, but too temporary and product-launch-heavy for this permanent page. Apple's own in-app-event guidance defines those events as timely, discoverable experiences, which is another reason to keep the event layer removable rather than make it the page's identity.

References:

- [Doist: Our History](https://handbook.doist.com/s/a772e0a3-2196-4568-b08d-c5b939653b7c/doc/our-history-5X74kRZbMT)
- [Messenger's 10th birthday](https://about.fb.com/news/2021/08/messenger-10th-birthday/)

## 6. Recommended page structure

The page should feel complete at roughly ten or eleven purposeful sections. It should not attempt to include all 124 version records, every current feature, every review, or a full company biography.

### Section 1: Hero — “Ten years of clearer money between people”

**Job:** establish the milestone, original purpose, and present relevance on the first mobile screen.

**Exact points:**

- Launched August 26, 2016.
- Began with a focused idea: keep the record clear.
- Now supports everyday IOUs through separate loans and repayment plans.
- The goal stayed the same.

**Content / visual:** the anniversary “10” artwork, the first-screen copy above, and an in-page `See the ten-year journey` action.

**Product bridge:** none beyond identifying what the app does. A campaign-only `Open the 10-year experience` action may be secondary.

**Exclude:** sale pricing, “10 DAYS ONLY,” rating counts, a full App Store badge, confetti animation, or an exhaustive feature list.

### Section 2: Temporary campaign card — “The anniversary experience is live”

**Job:** connect the permanent web story to the time-limited native celebration without changing the page's core meaning.

**Exact points during the campaign:**

- The celebration begins August 26.
- The anniversary offer ends September 4.
- The App Store event and offer dates must be described separately if their configured end times differ.
- The native screen is a thank-you experience for paid users and includes an upgrade offer only for eligible free users.

**Content / artifact:** a compact event card with two routes: open the native anniversary experience and view the App Store event/fallback.

**Product bridge / CTA:** `Open the 10-year experience`; secondary `View the App Store event`. If offer wording is present, use `See the anniversary offer in the app`, not `Buy now`.

**Exclude:** a countdown timer, prices that vary by storefront, urgency repeated elsewhere, or language implying every visitor is eligible.

**Evergreen state:** remove this entire card after the campaign or replace it with one quiet retrospective sentence. Do not leave an expired promotion embedded in the permanent narrative.

### Section 3: Factual proof strip — “A real decade of maintenance”

**Job:** turn the milestone into concise, verifiable evidence before the longer story.

**Recommended proof points:**

- `Launched August 26, 2016`
- `Maintained through ten years of iOS change`
- `Available in 10 languages today`
- `Version 7 in its tenth year` or a softer `More actively developed than ever`

The last phrase needs adjacent evidence. A more durable alternative is:

> In the months before the anniversary, the app added Loan Records, Repayment Plans, Timeline, Group Paybacks, Live Link, Balance Sync, Spaces, supporting photos, and more.

**Product bridge:** none. This is proof, not conversion copy.

**Exclude:** private paid-plan counts, download totals, commit counts, archive counts, “185 releases,” or unsupported user-growth language. `124 App Store version records` can be kept in the detailed chronology or editorial notes, but it is too technical for the main proof strip.

### Section 4: The enduring promise — “The app changed. The goal did not.”

**Job:** give meaning to the chronology before presenting dates.

**Exact points:**

- Money between friends, family, roommates, partners, and clients becomes difficult when memory is the system.
- The original app kept a person, entries, and one running balance together.
- Each later capability solved a deeper version of the same problem: what happened, what remains, and what comes next.
- You Owe Me remains a calm record and communication layer—not a bank, lender, payment processor, debt collector, accounting system, legal contract, or tax tool.

**Practical element:** a compact three-part model: `What happened → What remains → What happens next`.

**Soft product bridge:** none yet. This model should work as useful guidance even without the app.

**Exclude:** founder mythology, “revolutionizing finance,” competitive attacks, or a feature grid.

### Section 5: Main artifact — “The Clarity Ledger: 2016 → 2026”

**Job:** supply the page's original, standalone value: a curated, source-backed product history organized by user need rather than release trivia.

Use six eras. For each era, show three short fields:

1. `What people needed`
2. `What You Owe Me added`
3. `What stayed true`

#### 2016 — Remember the balance

- **Need:** stop relying on memory for who paid, who borrowed, and what remained.
- **Added:** people, entries, currencies, dates, and a running balance.
- **Stayed true:** one understandable record with each person.

#### 2019 — Keep the record useful over time

- **Need:** repeat bills, carry history forward, and take records beyond one moment.
- **Added:** recurring entries, exports, shareable history, and cloud continuity.
- **Stayed true:** the record should be usable when someone returns to it later.

#### 2022 — Stay clear through platform change

- **Need:** keep a long-lived app familiar and reliable as iOS and devices changed.
- **Added:** platform modernization, dark mode, clearer date grouping, and clearer direction guidance.
- **Stayed true:** technology should not make the money story harder to read.

#### 2024 — Rebuild the modern foundation

- **Need:** faster navigation, clearer direction, better performance, and a stronger base for more complex records.
- **Added:** the modern person/start experience, clearer lent/borrowed language, performance work, currency improvements, sharing, and accessibility-minded visual cues.
- **Stayed true:** complexity should stay behind a calm everyday interface.

#### 2025 — Make the next action clearer

- **Need:** know when to follow up, capture records faster, handle recurring or interest-bearing situations, and say the next thing calmly.
- **Added:** stronger reminders, voice entry, interest handling, Split Entry, exports/statements, and more contextual communication.
- **Stayed true:** a useful record should reduce awkwardness, not intensify it.

#### 2026 — Give complex arrangements structure

- **Need:** keep important loans separate, build a plan around real repayments, understand the relationship timeline, coordinate shared costs, and share or sync clearer records.
- **Added:** Loan Records, Repayment Plans, Timeline, Group Paybacks, Live Link, Balance Sync, Spaces, supporting photos, and ten-language support.
- **Stayed true:** every added layer should make the current balance and next step easier to understand.

**Visual / interaction:** a highly scannable vertical timeline on mobile, with no requirement to open every era. Progressive detail may be helpful, but an elaborate interactive experience is not required.

**Soft product bridge:** a single contextual link after 2026: `Explore what You Owe Me can do today` → Features.

**Exclude:** every patch version, exact feature-launch days when only repository commit evidence exists, technical migration details beyond what explains longevity, and historical interface claims without screenshots.

### Section 6: Flagship evolution story — “From ‘who owes what?’ to a clear path to paid back”

**Job:** make the ten-year evolution tangible through the strongest modern capability rather than another broad feature list.

**Exact points:**

- A running balance is enough for simple IOUs and everyday reimbursements.
- An important or long-running loan needs its own identity, remaining balance, notes, due date, interest context, repayments, and adjustments while still counting in the total with that person.
- A Repayment Plan turns that Loan Record into a schedule with weekly, biweekly, or monthly options.
- Real repayments can be partial, early, extra, or late; the schedule should remain connected to what actually happened.
- This is still a personal record and communication aid. It does not lend, collect, move money, or create a two-party legal contract.

**Example:** one restrained “moving deposit” scenario:

> In 2016, it could be one entry in a running balance. In 2026, it can be a separate Loan Record with a repayment plan, real payment history, next-payment context, reminders, and a shareable PDF—without losing the total relationship balance.

**Visual:** a three-screen sequence using current assets: Separate Loan Records → Choose a Payback Plan → Relationship Timeline. Prefer close crops of the meaningful UI over full App Store marketing panels where possible.

**Soft product bridge:** `See loans and repayment plans` → the current Features anchor or personal-loan solution page. Do not place a direct App Store CTA inside the explanation unless the visitor has already chosen this use case.

**Exclude:** a complete loan-feature inventory, interest calculations, legal claims, promises that two people are signing the same plan, or language that sounds like debt collection.

### Section 7: Practical value — “What ten years taught us about keeping money clear”

**Job:** make the page useful even to someone who never downloads the app.

Use a four-part clarity check:

1. **Name what happened.** Record the person, amount, date, direction, and reason in language both people could understand.
2. **Separate what matters.** Keep an important loan distinct from everyday shared expenses, even if both contribute to one total relationship balance.
3. **Record real repayments.** A plan is only useful when partial, early, extra, or changed payments update the story rather than live in a separate memory.
4. **Keep the next step visible.** The record should make the remaining balance, next date or action, and calm message easier to see.

Add a small “When a note is enough / when an ongoing record helps” comparison:

| A message or note may be enough | An ongoing record becomes useful |
|---|---|
| One small amount, one expected repayment, little chance of confusion | Multiple entries or partial repayments |
| Both people already agree on the amount and timing | A due date, recurring cost, interest, or repayment schedule matters |
| No history will be needed later | Either person may need to revisit what happened |
| No reminder or follow-up is likely | The next step, reminder, or update needs to stay visible |

**Soft product bridge:** one sentence after the framework: “You can use these principles in a note, spreadsheet, or message. You Owe Me becomes useful when the record needs to keep changing over time.”

**Useful next action:** `Try the running balance calculator` for a visitor who wants a browser-only tool.

**App CTA:** none in this section. The point is to prove that the site is willing to recommend a simpler method when it is sufficient.

**Exclude:** a downloadable worksheet, calculator, or second major interactive tool. Those would distract from the historical artifact and duplicate existing tools.

### Section 8: Customer proof — “Built for records people return to”

**Job:** validate the history through long-term reliance and continuous improvement, not generic praise.

Use two or at most three reviews:

- “This app impresses me more and more. It keeps evolving and improving.” — Raismotor
- “It's taken all the stress out of tracking money between family.” — BorrowedSun
- Optional third: “It's the only app where the developer truly listens and responds.” — iPepeep

Each review should have a short editorial label explaining its role: `Continued improvement`, `Less family stress`, `Built by listening`.

**Soft product bridge:** `Read real App Store reviews` → Reviews.

**Exclude:** the 500+ rating summary as the focus, a carousel of many reviews, generated testimonials, stock portraits, or reviews chosen only because they use loan keywords.

### Section 9: Present tense and future — “Ten years old, and still being built”

**Job:** prevent the page from feeling like a retrospective for a finished product.

**Exact points:**

- The most substantial expansion happened recently, especially in 2025–2026.
- This is evidence of present stewardship, not a promise of an unannounced roadmap.
- Customer support, reviews, suggestions, and long-term use helped make continued development possible.
- The app can be more capable without abandoning simplicity as the standard.

Recommended language:

> The tenth year was not a victory lap. It became the app's most active period of development, with deeper records for loans and repayment, clearer timelines and reminders, new ways to share or sync, and continued reliability work underneath them.

Optional creator note, only if the product is comfortable being explicit about its independent origin:

> A short signed paragraph can humanize the decade and explain why the project remained focused. It should thank customers and state the product standard; it should not become a founder biography.

**Product bridge:** none until the Best Next Step module.

**Exclude:** roadmap promises, “we are just getting started,” unsupported audience growth, private revenue/subscription figures, or development-volume bragging.

### Section 10: Best Next Step — “Choose what you want to understand next”

**Job:** help a now-informed visitor select the right depth of evaluation rather than pushing everyone directly to the App Store.

Recommended four routes:

1. **How it works** — `See the core You Owe Me model` → Quick Start.
2. **What it does today** — `Explore current features` → Features.
3. **Trust before installing** — `Read privacy and data details` → Privacy & Data.
4. **Use the app** — `Open You Owe Me on the App Store` → default App Store product page.

This is a stronger anniversary router than use-case cards. The visitor arrived for product trust and history, not necessarily with a single financial scenario already selected.

### Section 11: Closing thank-you — “Thank you for being part of the record”

**Job:** close emotionally after the visitor has already been given useful routes.

Recommended direction:

> Thank you for every entry recorded, every repayment marked, every review, every suggestion, and every year You Owe Me stayed on your phone. The app is ten years old—and still getting better.

Use one inclusive public message. Do not attempt paid/free personalization on the website unless the site can genuinely know the visitor's state.

**CTA:** one calm App Store badge may appear here, especially after the campaign. During the campaign, the native anniversary action can remain secondary.

**Exclude:** repeating the offer, adding another four-link router, or asking for a review and a download and an upgrade at the same time.

## 7. Practical or UX artifact

### Recommended artifact: “The Clarity Ledger: 2016 → 2026”

This is a source-backed, six-era timeline in which every milestone is structured as:

> What people needed → what You Owe Me added → what stayed true.

It is more valuable than a decorative timeline because it explains product judgment. A visitor can see not only that the app accumulated features, but that each era addressed a deeper form of the same human problem. The final field—what stayed true—keeps the story from becoming a release list and gives the visitor a standard they can apply to any record system.

It materially improves the page because it:

- Provides original first-party history unavailable elsewhere.
- Converts ten years into evidence that can be scanned and cited.
- Demonstrates maintenance across quieter and more active eras honestly.
- Explains why recent complexity does not represent mission drift.
- Creates a reason to return, share, or reference the permanent URL.
- Gives search and answer systems explicit dates, feature eras, and product boundaries.

It belongs on a standalone URL because it is too substantial for the homepage, too historical for Features, and too product-led for Reviews.

### Interaction recommendation

Do not build a calculator or game for the anniversary. The main value is evidence, not input/output. A responsive vertical timeline with optional progressive detail is enough. The visitor should never need to interact to understand the six eras; interaction may reveal supporting detail, but it must not hide the core story.

The supporting four-part clarity check in Section 7 makes the page practically useful. It should not compete with the timeline as a second large artifact.

## 8. CTA and product bridge plan

The page should have fewer conversion actions than Features because its job is to establish trust. Each action should appear only when the visitor has a corresponding need.

| Placement | Exact working wording | Type | User need served | Destination / route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | `See the ten-year journey` | Useful page action | Understand why the milestone matters | In-page timeline anchor |
| Hero, campaign only | `Open the 10-year experience` | App deep link | Existing or returning user wants the native celebration | `youoweme://events/anniversary` with a web/App Store fallback |
| Campaign card | `View the App Store event` | App Store event action | Visitor wants Apple's event page or needs to install first | Anniversary event URL, after the date discrepancy is resolved |
| End of timeline | `Explore what You Owe Me can do today` | Content action | Visitor understands history and now wants current breadth | `/features/` |
| Loan evolution | `See loans and repayment plans` | Solution / feature action | Visitor specifically cares about structured personal loans | Prefer the relevant website solution or Features anchor first |
| Practical framework | `Try the running balance calculator` | Useful tool action | Visitor wants a browser-only way to clarify a simple balance | `/tools/running-balance-calculator/` |
| Customer proof | `Read real App Store reviews` | Trust action | Visitor wants independent customer evidence | `/reviews/` |
| Best Next Step | `See the core You Owe Me model` | Guide action | Visitor wants setup understanding | `/quick-start/` |
| Best Next Step | `Read privacy and data details` | Trust action | Visitor is not ready to install before understanding data handling | `/privacy-and-data/` |
| Best Next Step / close | `Open You Owe Me on the App Store` or standard badge | Default App Store action | Visitor is ready for the general product | Default product page |
| Campaign card, eligible users only in app | `See the anniversary offer in the app` | Offer bridge | Visitor intentionally wants the temporary offer | Native anniversary experience; do not promise eligibility on web |

### Default App Store versus Custom Product Page

The permanent anniversary page represents the whole product and should use the default App Store page, not a loan-, family-, roommate-, or debt-specific Custom Product Page. Routing the main CTA to a narrowly framed CPP would contradict the page's broad ten-year story.

The Loan Records section should first route to the corresponding website feature/solution page. If a direct App Store CTA is later added specifically inside that section, the long-term-balances CPP could be evaluated, but it is not automatically the right match for the anniversary narrative. Default recommendation: no direct CPP action on this page.

### Where no app CTA is needed

- The proof strip.
- The enduring-promise section.
- Individual timeline eras.
- The practical clarity framework.
- The customer-review cards themselves.

These sections establish usefulness and trust. Adding App Store actions inside them would make the page feel measured by click density rather than reader progress.

## 9. Best Next Step placement

Place the Best Next Step module **after Section 9, “Ten years old, and still being built,” and before the final thank-you**.

At that point the visitor has:

- Understood the original product purpose.
- Seen evidence across ten years.
- Seen the strongest current evolution example.
- Received a practical non-app framework.
- Read customer proof.
- Seen that the project is active today.

The trust question is complete, so asking the visitor to choose a next route is useful rather than interruptive.

Placing it immediately after the hero would be weaker because it would route visitors away before the page proves its unique value. Placing it after the closing thank-you would be weaker because the emotional conclusion would appear to end the page, and the router would feel bolted on. The final thank-you should remain the last editorial impression, with at most one calm App Store action.

## 10. Image plan

### Primary hero asset

Use the existing native anniversary artwork:

`anniversary_2026_hero.png`

It shows a large sculpted “10” built from people, record cards, and borrowed/lent indicators in the app's green, cream, and gold palette. ## 13. Risks and guardrails

### Duplicate-content risk

**Risk:** repeating the homepage proposition, Features inventory, or Reviews proof wall.

**Guardrail:** every section must answer one of four anniversary questions: what began, what changed, what stayed true, or why the project is alive now. If a block only says what a feature does today, link to Features instead.

### Search-intent risk

**Risk:** forcing generic terms such as `loan tracker app`, `IOU app`, or `debt repayment tracker` into the page could cannibalize solution and tool pages while weakening the branded history intent.

**Guardrail:** target the entity and milestone naturally: You Owe Me history, launched in 2016, ten-year anniversary, product evolution, actively maintained. Let deeper pages own task keywords.

### Thin-content or padding risk

**Risk:** an anniversary can become a hero, a thank-you paragraph, and a sale banner stretched into a page.

**Guardrail:** the Clarity Ledger is the required original artifact. Conversely, do not pad it with all 124 versions, every feature, ten arbitrary lessons, or ten review quotes merely because the number is ten.

### Tone and relationship-safety risk

**Risk:** celebratory money language can become triumphant, aggressive, or insensitive to people managing awkward loans and family arrangements.

**Guardrail:** emphasize clarity, reduced misunderstanding, gratitude, and calm next steps. Avoid guilt, collection language, public-shaming metaphors, or implying that repayment problems are moral failures.

### Legal, lending, collection, tax, and accounting drift

**Risk:** the loans section may accidentally present the app as a lender, payment processor, debt collector, contract, amortization authority, tax record, or accounting system.

**Guardrail:** state once, plainly, that the app records and communicates; it does not lend, collect, move money, or create a shared legal agreement. Do not imply that Balance Sync makes a Repayment Plan a synchronized contract.

### Claim and analytics risk

**Risk:** “growing and thriving” could be interpreted as rising downloads or audience size, which the available year-over-year acquisition data does not support.

**Guardrail:** attach growth to product capability, development activity, or customer support. Prefer `more actively developed than ever` and substantiate it with the recent product expansion. Keep exact paid-plan, download, commit, and archive figures private unless a separate publication decision is made.

### Historical accuracy risk

**Risk:** repository implementation dates can precede public release, historical App Store notes are incomplete, and no verified 2016 UI screenshot is currently in the web asset set.

**Guardrail:** describe older changes by year or era; distinguish repository evidence from public release evidence; do not fabricate old interface visuals; preserve the sourcebook as the editorial fact base.

### Campaign-date risk

**Risk:** the supplied offer end date is September 4, while the public App Store event payload observed on August 24 reported a later event end time.

**Guardrail:** verify App Store Connect before publishing event dates. The page can safely say the offer ends September 4 while separately describing the App Store event if its visibility window differs.

### Overpromotion and CTA-density risk

**Risk:** multiple App Store badges, offer prompts, and “open in app” links would make the thank-you feel transactional—especially to paid customers.

**Guardrail:** one in-page history action in the hero, one removable campaign card, one late Best Next Step module, and at most one final App Store action. No app CTA is needed inside the timeline or practical framework.

### High-traffic / low-quality risk

**Risk:** generic `10 year anniversary` content could attract celebration-template or corporate-anniversary traffic unrelated to personal money records.

**Guardrail:** keep the title, opening, and artifact explicitly about You Owe Me and money between people. Do not add generic anniversary ideas, business-longevity advice, or “10 lessons for startups.”

### Elements that must remain out of scope

- A full public changelog.
- A launch-by-launch archive of all version identifiers.
- Exact private subscription, revenue, download, commit, or archive statistics.
- A new calculator or anniversary game.
- A general company/founder biography.
- A roadmap or promises about unannounced features.
- A comparative “best app” claim.
- Payment processing, lending, collection, legal, tax, or accounting advice.
- Personalized paid/free messaging without reliable visitor state.
- Fake historical screenshots or staged customer photos.
- Permanent sale copy in the evergreen narrative.

## 14. Success definition

Success should be evaluated in two layers: the ten-day campaign and the permanent trust page.

### Campaign success

- Visitors reach and engage with the anniversary story rather than only the offer.
- Existing users use `Open the 10-year experience` and reach the correct native screen.
- The App Store event receives relevant event impressions, event-page views, app opens, downloads, and other available event-attributed actions.
- Eligible free users reach the offer intentionally, without paid users being made to feel sold to.
- Campaign clicks can be distinguished between native deep link, App Store event, and default App Store fallback.

Apple documents event impressions, event-page views, app opens, downloads, subscriptions, and sales as available in-app-event analytics; those should be interpreted as campaign signals, not as proof that the permanent page itself succeeded. See [App Store Connect Analytics: In-App Events](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect-analytics/acquisition/in-app-events/).

### Evergreen success

- Relevant branded impressions appear for queries about You Owe Me's history, age, launch year, maintenance, or anniversary.
- Search-result CTR shows that the title and description satisfy those trust questions.
- Visitors reach and meaningfully view the Clarity Ledger, especially the 2026 era and flagship loan evolution.
- Internal movement goes to the next page that matches the reader's unresolved concern: Features, Quick Start, Reviews, Privacy & Data, the running-balance tool, or the loan solution.
- App Store movement occurs after evidence and fit have been established, not merely because a badge was placed high on the page.
- The page contributes assisted App Store visits and qualified downloads when it appears earlier in a multi-page path.
- Existing customers share or revisit the page, and external sites or answer systems cite its launch date or product history.
- The page remains useful after the offer and App Store event end, with no stale urgency or expired dates in the main narrative.

### Interpretive cautions

- A low direct-download rate is not automatically failure; a trust page may correctly send cautious visitors to Privacy, Reviews, or Quick Start first.
- A high campaign click rate is not automatically success if almost nobody reads the history and the page behaves like a disguised sale panel.
- Raw pageviews are weak without relevant entry queries, useful engagement, internal movement, event/app actions, citations, or assisted conversion.
- Do not invent a numerical target before establishing a baseline for the existing “maintained since 2016” proof points and comparable trust pages.

## Final editorial test

Before approving the eventual copy, read the page without the campaign card and without any App Store CTA. It should still leave a visitor with four clear conclusions:

1. You Owe Me has a verifiable history beginning on August 26, 2016.
2. It is actively maintained and substantially more capable in 2026.
3. Its complexity grew in service of one consistent idea: make money between people clearer.
4. The visitor now has a useful framework—and an informed choice about what to explore next.

If the page only works when the sale is visible, the strategy has failed. If it still works in 2027 as the authoritative story of the app's first decade, it has succeeded.
